# Retention mechanics and gambling-style harms: shaping Bahlou's meta-game

Reading conventions. "[older]" means published before 2022. "[snippet]" means the claim comes from a search result excerpt and the page itself was not read in full or could not be opened; verify before quoting. Everything else was read at the source. Legal points are research notes, not legal advice.

## How do Duolingo streaks, leagues, friend quests and streak freezes work, and what has Duolingo published?

### Takeaway
Duolingo's best-documented wins come from making the streak easier to keep and softer to lose, and the published lifts are small but measured. Its social features are reported mostly through adoption figures and adjectives, with no published retention effect.

### Cited Findings
Streak rules and experiments
- [older, 2020] A/B test: separating the streak from the daily goal, so that one lesson extends the streak, gave +3.3% Day 14 retention, +1% daily active learners, and +10.5% share of daily learners on a streak within 20 days (+19% among new learners). Daily-goal completion fell, but total learning time rose. The authors concluded that lowering the barrier to a daily habit mattered more than volume, at least early on — [Duolingo blog, Improving the streak](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak)
- [older, 2020] Before that change, nearly 40% of learners who were active two days running without a streak had the highest daily goal, showing that demanding goals broke streaks — [same source](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak)
- [older, 2020] About a year later, just over half of daily learners had a 7+ day streak, up from about a third. Learners with a 7-day streak were 2.4 times more likely to return the next day than those without a streak. This comparison is observational — [same source](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak)
- 2022: Allowing learners to equip up to two Streak Freezes at a time raised relative daily active learners by +0.38%, and Duolingo says it did not encourage more days off. New streak animations raised 7-day retention of new learners by +1.7%. Learners reaching a 7-day streak were 3.6 times more likely to complete their course (observational). The post attributes the streak's pull to loss aversion, without data on that mechanism. A refill of Freezes was shown at 400 gems — [Duolingo blog, How the streak builds habit](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit)
- [older, 2017, page later updated] Streak Wager: spend in-game currency to commit to 7 more days, and success doubles the stake. Retention rose significantly at Day 1, 7 and 14, peaking at +14% on Day 7, baseline not given — [Duolingo blog, How streaks keep learners committed](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-streaks-keep-duolingo-learners-committed-to-their-language-goals). A secondary blog attributes the 14% to Day 14, which conflicts with Duolingo's own post; use Duolingo's figure.
- [older, 2017] Weekend Amulet, a store item protecting the streak over the weekend: users were 4% more likely to return a week later and 5% less likely to lose their streak. Weekend activity falls by "as much as 5-10%". Learners who binged lessons were much more likely to abandon the app than those who paced themselves — [same source](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-streaks-keep-duolingo-learners-committed-to-their-language-goals)

Social and leagues
- Q2 2024 letter: Friend Streak lets a user share a streak with up to five friends and nudge those who have not done their lesson. The only performance claim is "Early results have been excellent". Over half of DAUs follow at least one friend. One in five DAUs has a streak over 365 days. Friends Quests and Leaderboards are said to bring learners back more often, with no figure given. The family plan "has higher retention than individual plans", also with no figure — [Duolingo Q2 2024 shareholder letter (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000156208824000203/q2fy24duolingo06-30x24shar.htm)
- [snippet] One third of DAUs had a Friend Streak in Q4 2024. The filing page returned 404 when fetched — [Q4 2024 letter copy](https://capedge.com/filing/1562088/0001562088-25-000039/DUOL-8K/file/3)
- [older, snippet] Duolingo cites adding five leagues to the Leaderboard as an output of experimentation, and the same post reports a subscription promotion that lifted revenue slightly but lowered retention — [Duolingo blog, Improving Duolingo one experiment at a time](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-duolingo-one-experiment-at-a-time)
- [snippet, third-party analysis, no Duolingo data] Promotion is quick in early tiers and scarce at the top, and demotion pressure makes weekly engagement matter — [Deconstructor of Fun, Duolingo leagues](https://duolingo.deconstructoroffun.com/mechanics/leagues)

### Inferences
- The strongest transferable lesson is to make the minimum qualifying action small. One casino session or one mission should be enough, and no minimum wager should be required to keep a streak.
- Bahlou's streak should qualify on a non-wagering action, otherwise the streak pays people to gamble fictional money daily.
- Duolingo's percentages come from very large samples. With 7-30 users, Bahlou cannot A/B test and should treat these as design hints only.
- The Wager's mechanic is commitment with a stake, but it stakes currency on one's own behaviour rather than on chance, which makes it a good model for replacing chance-based bets.

### Gaps
- No published Duolingo effect size for Friends Quests, Friend Streak or leagues was found. Marketing blogs quote figures (for example, 36% DAU growth and 28% churn) with no primary citation, so they are excluded.
- Duolingo does not publish negative effects such as streak anxiety.
- The current Friends Quests rules could not be confirmed from a primary source.

## How do Clash Royale, Marvel Snap, battle passes and seasons structure progression, and what do they show about inflation and sinks?

### Takeaway
Passes work when a game has continuing demand for what they give out; once a collection is capped, rewards lose value and undercut other sources. Time-gating a single currency is the common pacing tool, and evidence here is mostly industry commentary aimed at revenue.

### Cited Findings
- [older, about 2020] Battle pass success depends on "content depth". Clash of Clans' resource-depletion economy offers many sinks, so a pass fit well. Clash Royale's collection caps around unit level 13-14, discounted pass pulls undercut full-price pulls, and clan trade tokens that swap exact card copies made this worse. The author judged that pass not revenue-accretive. Cosmetic passes such as Fortnite's need a constant pipeline of desirable cosmetics — [Deconstructor of Fun, Michail Katkoff](http://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/supercell-battle-pass-clash-of-clans-vs-clash-royale)
- [snippet, 2023] Counterpoint: Pass Royale 2.0 and a concurrent expansion coincided with +41% month-on-month revenue, so a later redesign may have changed the picture — [Liquid and Grit](https://www.liquidandgrit.com/may-2023-core-report/)
- 2022 launch-era analysis of Marvel Snap: Credits are time-gated (500 a day from freebies and missions, 1,250 a week from the Weekly Challenge) and are "the limiting factor" on collection progress, whereas Boosters are earned uncapped and should never bottleneck. After Collector Level 500, reward chances shift into caches at about 50% then about 25% for a card. Estimated about 7 months to complete a collection free. Season pass non-buyers get the exclusive card two months later — [Daniel Friedman, Marvel Snap economy guide](https://danielfriedman.substack.com/p/a-comprehensive-guide-to-the-marvel)
- [snippet] Marvel Snap has added further gold sinks intentionally, to discourage optimising only for game pieces — [Snap.fan](https://snap.fan/news/why-is-marvel-snap-so-expensive/)
- [snippet] Marvel Snap runs three seasons over 13 weeks (two 4-week and one 5-week) — [Marvel Snap Zone](https://marvelsnapzone.com/marvel-snap-economy-pt-1-four-ways-card-acquisition-is-changing/)
- 2025 preprint on Overwatch 2's free-to-play shift identifies nine mechanics using deceptive design, including grinding and pay-to-skip within battle-pass-style progression. [snippet] It also cites the FTC's 2022 action against Epic over dark patterns driving unwanted Fortnite purchases — [arXiv 2503.22901](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.22901v1)

### Inferences
- A collection with sets has the Clash Royale problem built in: once sets are complete, cases are worthless. Bahlou needs seasonal sets, or cosmetics that retire, to keep demand alive.
- Marvel Snap's pattern applies directly: one time-gated currency sets the pace, so grinding casino games cannot accelerate progression.
- In a free app with no revenue, passes need not create scarcity through expiry. A season track that lets late joiners catch up preserves the structure and removes the FOMO that the deceptive-design literature criticises.

### Gaps
- No reliable published data was found comparing inflation or sink rates across these games, and developer talks at GDC on these specific economies were not located.
- No study was found measuring player wellbeing against Fortnite-style seasonal FOMO. A student thesis and opinion guides exist but were not used.

## What keeps a virtual economy healthy in a small population?

### Takeaway
There is little rigorous evidence for economies of 7-30 people, so this section is mainly practitioner guidance and reasoning. The core rule is that faucets must be tied to population and time, with sinks in place from the start, and that total supply should be tracked per head.

### Cited Findings
- [snippet, practitioner blogs, not individually verified] Inflation arises when currency enters faster than it leaves, designers commonly fail by adding sinks late, operators track money supply per active user, and surplus is better absorbed by attractive expensive sinks such as status cosmetics than by cutting income, which causes backlash — [Designing game economies](https://medium.com/@msahinn21/designing-game-economies-inflation-resource-management-and-balance-fa1e6c894670); [Game Economy Design](https://metavert.io/game-economy-design); [Ten Ton Hammer on sandbox MMOs](https://www.tentonhammer.com/articles/how-virtual-economies-handle-inflation-in-modern-sandbox-mmos.amp)
- [snippet, weak source: player forum opinion] Velocity of circulation matters as well as money supply when judging inflation — [EVE Online forum thread](https://forums.eveonline.com/t/equalization-of-isk-sink-and-faucets/19140)
- [older] In Clash Royale, peer-to-peer card trading through clan tokens reduced the value of other acquisition routes, according to one analyst — [Deconstructor of Fun](http://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/supercell-battle-pass-clash-of-clans-vs-clash-royale)

### Inferences (design reasoning, not empirical findings)
- At this size, one hoarder or one lucky streak can hold most of the money. Track the median balance and the top holder's share rather than the average.
- Bahlou has many faucets: streak, chest, bailout, missions, achievements, weekly prizes, school work and cases. Each should have a daily or weekly cap, and bailouts should restore only a floor amount, so that going broke does not pay.
- Candidate sinks: seasonal cosmetics with rising prices, name or title rentals, tournament entry in fictional chips, a house fee on friend-to-friend bets, and upgrade costs for frames.
- A seasonal soft reset (for example, converting balance above a threshold into a season cosmetic or a title) handles inflation and gives latecomers a fair start.
- Auctions in a group this small risk the richest player winning everything or two friends colluding. Sealed-bid auctions with a per-season win limit reduce this. Use them only for cosmetic rarities.
- Direct transfers enable collusion and pressure between friends, and make any out-of-app selling easier. Keep them capped, logged and visible, or restrict them to gifting of cosmetics. See the France section for why any real-money resale matters.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed study or published postmortem on virtual economies with fewer than about 100 users.
- GDC Vault talks on faucets and sinks were not found through search.
- No evidence was found on betting between friends in fictional currency as an economy mechanic.

## What makes cooperative or guild goals outperform solo goals?

### Takeaway
Shared goals are well supported for cohesion and staying together, but the claim that co-op simply outperforms competition is not established. The one strong randomized trial located favoured competition for lasting behaviour. Both are likely useful in different roles.

### Cited Findings
- [older, snippet] In World of Warcraft, players tend to stay together across time and guild changes; on average 2/3 of characters seen week to week and across four-week periods stayed in the same guild — [Play Together, Stay Together](https://scholars.cityu.edu.hk/en/publications/play-together-stay-together-community-cohesion-and-stability-in-a/)
- [older, 2014, snippet] In EverQuest II, relationships began transient and grew more durable; level similarity, shared guild membership and geographic proximity helped preserve them — [Shen, Monge and Williams, via JASIST record](https://zendy.io/title/10.1002/asi.23129)
- [older, snippet] Strong intragroup ties aid cohesion but can fragment a guild if a strong subgroup leaves — [Death of a guild, birth of a network](https://scholars.cityu.edu.hk/en/publications/death-of-a-guild-birth-of-a-network-online-community-ties-within-/)
- Duolingo's Friend Streak relies on accountability to friends as well as to oneself, with up to five people and nudges, but publishes no effect size — [Q2 2024 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000156208824000203/q2fy24duolingo06-30x24shar.htm)
- [older, 2019, snippet; the source page returned 404 on fetch] STEP UP randomized trial of gamified step goals with control, support, collaboration and competition arms, 24 weeks plus 12 weeks follow-up. Competition placed people in groups of three, ranked weekly. No significant difference between gamification arms during the intervention. In follow-up only competition stayed significantly above control (6,592 vs 5,899 steps). Collaboration and support did not — [ACC summary of STEP UP](https://acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/articles/2019/11/26/08/23/key-takeaways-regarding-gamification-to-increase-physical-activity)
- [snippet] Among soccer teams, cooperative gamification fostered enjoyment, social identity and aspects of cohesion, while intra-team competition did not harm group dynamics — [University of Bamberg record](https://fis.uni-bamberg.de/handle/uniba/116558)

### Inferences
- STEP UP's small ranked groups of three resemble Bahlou's real size. Small friend groups with recurring competition can outlast collaboration in steps, but the domain differs and transfer is uncertain.
- Bahlou should run both: a whole-group weekly goal for cohesion (everyone benefits, nobody excluded), and light competitive leaderboards.
- With 7-30 people, a permanent clan system would split a friend group and risks the fragmentation pattern above. Rotating teams drawn each week, or teams matched by school year, are safer.
- The shared goal must count non-wagering actions, or it will pressure friends to gamble more to help the group.
- Friend nudges should be opt-in and capped, since social pressure among real friends is stronger than among strangers.

### Gaps
- No study directly compared cooperative against solo goals on app retention in small closed friend groups.
- Clan or team chest impact data from Supercell or others was not found.
- Whether Duolingo's social users retain better because of the feature, or because keen users add friends, is unresolved in published material.

## What does research say about social casino games, loot boxes, near-misses and migration? What about France?

### Takeaway
Associations between simulated gambling, loot box spending and real gambling are consistent, but causation is unproven and the literature is focused on paid mechanics. Bahlou's free currency probably removes the main legal trigger in France and some of the risk, but its audience is in the highest-prevalence age band, and its casino teaches the same activity.

### Cited Findings
Social casino
- 2026: US national probability sample of 1,677 people aged 13-27 (NORC AmeriSpeak), surveyed April to July 2026, 21 activities. 41% of social casino players were classed as high-risk problem gamblers, nearly four times the rate of those on other activities. Cross-sectional, instrument and subgroup size not reported in the release — [Rutgers School of Social Work](https://socialwork.rutgers.edu/news/2026-us-prevalence-study-finds-high-rates-problem-gambling-among-adolescents-and-young-adults)
- [older, 2015, snippet; PubMed page did not load on fetch] About 26% of social casino gamers reported migrating to online gambling, with micro-transactions the only unique predictor — [PubMed 25410278](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25410278/)
- [older, 2017, snippet] Focus groups with 21 young-adult online gamblers at two Canadian universities: several reported moving to online gambling soon after social casino play; others said it depended on the person — [PubMed 28092197](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28092197); [related qualitative study on motivations](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5569650/)
- [snippet, 2023] Adolescents recounted that easy wins in social casino games fostered excitement and mistaken beliefs about winning chances. Simulated gambling is associated with disordered gaming, most strongly for social casino games and for spending real money on simulated gambling — [International Journal of Mental Health and Addiction](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11469-023-01119-6)

Loot boxes
- 2025: BMC Psychology replication, British Columbia, ages 19-25, 6-month follow-up, about 126 at baseline after heavy data cleaning, 54 non-gamblers analysed. 7 (12.9%) began gambling. Frequentist logistic models for initiation were non-significant (loot box spending OR 1.31, p = 0.118), Bayesian models with informed priors favoured an effect (OR 1.27, BF10 7.46). Loot box spending predicted later gambling spend (B 0.44, p = 0.005). Authors claim only temporal precedence and call the study exploratory. Attrition was 45.7%. Earned and paid boxes were not separated — [PMC12044733](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12044733/)
- [snippet] That paper cites a meta-analytic correlation of r = 0.26 between loot box spending and problem gambling — [PMC12044733](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12044733/)
- [snippet, 2023] Pre-registered Overwatch study: purchasers showed more spending harms, risky loot box use and impulsivity, but earners showed greater gambling-related harms. Authors advise assessing game by game — [Journal of Gambling Studies via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35834117). This is the most relevant study to Bahlou, because Overwatch boxes could be earned for free, but the abstract could not be opened directly.
- [older, 2020, snippet] Paying for loot boxes is linked to problem gambling irrespective of game features; cash-out, near-miss display and use of in-game currency may weakly strengthen the link — [Zendle et al. record](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/148267)
- [older, 2018, snippet] In 7,422 gamers, the link with problem gambling was stronger for loot box spending than for other in-game purchases — [Zendle and Cairns, PLOS ONE](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6248934/)
- [older, 2020, snippet, verify] A search summary reported that an Australian study found no evidence that earlier loot box experience predicts later gambling problems. The page's own headline says loot boxes "potentially groom" young people, so this finding may be misattributed and should not be quoted unchecked — [GambleAware NSW](https://gambleaware.nsw.gov.au/news-and-media/loot-boxes-potentially-groom-young-people-for-gambling)

Near-miss
- [older, 2019-2020] Pisklak, Yong and Spetch reviewed the near-miss literature and ran pigeon experiments and one human experiment (296 recruited, 178 reaching extinction). They could not reproduce increased persistence and questioned the theoretical basis. Limitations include no real money at stake and non-problem-gambler undergraduates — [Journal of Gambling Studies](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7214505)
- [older, snippet] Contrasting work found near-misses behave like wins in motivation and arousal, and increase persistence at moderate rates in simulations — [MetaTOC record](https://metatoc.com/papers/17103-learning-and-affect-following-near-miss-outcomes-in-simulated-gambling)

France
- The legal definition of gambling rests on four conditions: an offer to the public, an element of chance, a financial sacrifice and a hope of gain (art. L. 320-1 code de la sécurité intérieure), as stated in a parliamentary amendment of 30 September 2023 — [Assemblée nationale, amendment 969](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/16/amendements/1674/AN/969.pdf). The article was rewritten by the 2019 ordinance — [Légifrance, ordonnance 2019-1015](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/jorf/id/JORFSCTA000039167505)
- [older, 2014] For lotteries, the Hamon law added that a financial sacrifice exists when the organiser requires an advance payment, even if later reimbursable, and that the ban extends to skill-based games — [Squire Patton Boggs, La Revue](https://larevue.squirepattonboggs.com/Notion-de-loterie-nouvelle-definition-introduite-par-la-loi-Hamon_a2309.html). Article numbering should be rechecked after later reforms.
- 2022 legal commentary: loot boxes are not clearly caught in France because "gain" is treated in a patrimonial sense, and consumer-code rules on misleading practices may apply. By contrast, Belgium's gaming commission found in 2019 that loot boxes meet its game-of-chance test — [Le Mag Juridique](https://www.lemag-juridique.com/categories-dactus/divers/articles/reglementation-lootboxes-2971.htm)
- JONUM: SREN law (no. 2024-449, arts 40-41) and decree no. 2026-60, effective 7 February 2026, create a three-year trial launched by the ANJ on 19 February 2026. The cumulative criteria are financial sacrifice, chance, an online service and acquisition of monetisable digital objects — [ANJ](https://anj.fr/launch-jonum-regulatory-framework-trial)
- An object is monetisable if it can be sold for value to third parties other than the operator; euro winnings are excluded — [Squire Patton Boggs](https://larevue.squirepattonboggs.com/le-regime-experimental-des-jonum-jeux-a-objet-numeriques-monetisables.html). [snippet, secondary summaries] Casino mechanics such as roulette and slots are excluded from JONUM, and identity checks, self-limitation tools and health warnings are required — [same source](https://larevue.squirepattonboggs.com/le-regime-experimental-des-jonum-jeux-a-objet-numeriques-monetisables.html); [Dreyfus, 2026](https://www.dreyfus.fr/en/2026/06/29/what-is-the-legal-status-of-games-involving-monetisable-digital-objects-jonum/)
- [snippet, undated figure, attribution to be confirmed] A parliamentary amendment citing OFDT and ANJ work gives problem gambling prevalence of 10.1% among 18-24s, against 7.8% for 25-34s; the denominator is unclear and is likely gamblers rather than all young adults — [Assemblée nationale amendment](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/17/amendements/2797/AN/297.rtf)
- [snippet] The ANJ's survey published on 18 February 2026 of 5,000 15-17-year-olds found 42.6% had gambled in the past year, showing rising exposure just below Bahlou's age range — [Économie Matin](https://www.economiematin.fr/?p=213242)

### Inferences
- Legal reading for Bahlou, with moderate confidence: no purchase means no financial sacrifice, and no cash-out means no patrimonial gain, so at least two of four cumulative criteria appear absent. Invite-only status may also weaken "offer to the public", but I did not find authority on small private groups.
- That reading could change if any of the following occurs: real prizes for leaderboards or the tournament, any paid entry or donation tied to play, friends selling currency, cards or accounts for euros, or side bets in euros settled on app results. Rules should forbid these explicitly.
- Skill-based games are covered too once money is involved, so poker's skill element offers no legal shelter for real-money stakes.
- Evidence on harm centres on spending real money. Bahlou removes that, but the Overwatch earners result and the "easy wins" finding mean free play cannot be treated as harmless, particularly in the 18-25 age group.
- Near-miss evidence is mixed, so it is not proven to increase persistence. Since it has no legitimate benefit for a fair game, the simple choice is not to engineer it at all.
- Fair odds matter specifically: inflated free-play win rates could teach false beliefs, as the qualitative findings suggest. Bahlou's odds should match real-game odds or be plainly stated when they do not.

### Gaps
- I found no 2022-2026 longitudinal or experimental study isolating free, non-purchasable social casino play among young adults. Most of the migration evidence is older, cross-sectional or qualitative.
- A German longitudinal study of adolescents (Hayer and colleagues, 2018) is reported second-hand as finding that simulated gambling predicted later real-money gambling. I could not reach the paper, so it is not listed as a finding.
- I found no ANJ statement specifically on free social casinos with non-purchasable currency, or on private friend apps. The current text of arts L. 320-1 and L. 322-2 could not be retrieved from Légifrance, so a lawyer or the texts should confirm.
- I found no French case law on "offre au public" for a small invite-only group.
- No recent 18-24-specific ANJ survey was located; the figure above needs confirmation.

## What concrete safeguards do responsible designers use?

### Takeaway
Pop-up messages have the best quantified support, and effects are moderate and short-term. Other safeguards are standard practice with thinner evidence here, but in a free app they are cheap, and French rules for adjacent products show the expected baseline.

### Cited Findings
- [older, 2021] Meta-analysis of 18 studies on responsible gambling pop-ups: pooled g = 0.413 for cognitions and g = 0.505 for behaviour, with substantial unexplained heterogeneity, and effects described as short-term — [Bjørseth et al., Frontiers in Psychiatry](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7868407/)
- [older, snippet] The same literature lists self-exclusion, behavioural tracking, loss and deposit limits, pre-commitment and warning messages as tools, but the review gives no pooled effect for those — [same source](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7868407/)
- [snippet, secondary summaries] Even France's lighter JONUM framework requires identity and age checks, self-limitation tools and a health warning, and excludes casino mechanics — [Squire Patton Boggs](https://larevue.squirepattonboggs.com/le-regime-experimental-des-jonum-jeux-a-objet-numeriques-monetisables.html)
- [older, 2020, snippet] Cash-out, near-miss displays and in-game currency may weakly strengthen the link between loot boxes and problem gambling, so design features do appear to modulate risk, though only weakly — [Zendle et al.](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/148267)
- [older, 2017] Duolingo's finding that binging predicts abandonment supports pacing, independent of harm reduction — [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-streaks-keep-duolingo-learners-committed-to-their-language-goals)

### Inferences (recommended safeguards for Bahlou)
- Session reminder after a set play time, with a summary of chips won or lost during the session. Evidence supports short-term effect only, so pair it with hard limits.
- Daily loss limit in fictional chips with a cool-down period, plus user-set limits that can be lowered immediately and raised only after a delay.
- Odds and house edge displayed on every game and every case, and no fake inflated win rates.
- A visible self-exclusion or pause mode that freezes streaks and leaderboards, so stepping away costs nothing. No push notifications during a pause.
- A static link to Joueurs Info Service and a plain note that the app's odds do not make real gambling safe.
- No links, ads, affiliate codes or promotion of real-money operators in the app, and no gambling-themed rewards for real-money activity.

### Gaps
- I found no 2022-2026 systematic review on limit-setting or session reminders, or evidence on the effect of clear odds disclosure on behaviour.
- I found no published evaluation of responsible design features in free or social casino products.

## Which mechanics should Bahlou prioritise, adapt or restrict?

### Takeaway
Bahlou should centre its retention on friends, seasons and identity, and use casino play as a side activity rather than the source of progress. Priority goes to low-effort streaks with generous protection, whole-group goals and cosmetic seasons, with chance-based rewards limited.

### Cited Findings
- Low-friction streak qualification and protection have measured lifts — [Duolingo 2020](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak); [Duolingo 2022](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit)
- Reward depth has to continue past the collection cap — [Deconstructor of Fun](http://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/supercell-battle-pass-clash-of-clans-vs-clash-royale)
- Small ranked groups sustained behaviour in the one RCT located — [STEP UP summary, snippet](https://acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/articles/2019/11/26/08/23/key-takeaways-regarding-gamification-to-increase-physical-activity)
- Paid chance mechanics carry the most consistent harm association — [PMC12044733](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12044733/)

### Inferences (my synthesis; the strength rating reflects source quality)

| Mechanic | Evidence strength | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Streak with freeze | Good (A/B results) | Keep. Qualify on any one non-wager action. Allow 2+ freezes and a "weekend/exam" protection. |
| Friend or shared streak | Moderate (adoption only) | Add. Pairs or small groups, opt-in nudges, capped. |
| Whole-group weekly goal | Moderate for cohesion | Add. Count non-wager actions. Reward all. |
| Weekly leaderboard | Moderate (STEP UP, Duolingo) | Keep but rank on missions and non-wager points. Show several boards. Do not rank by chip profit alone. |
| Seasons and season track | Moderate, mostly commentary | Add, 4-8 week seasons aligned to the school calendar. Allow catch-up. |
| Cosmetics shop | Reasonable | Keep as main sink. Rotate, and add seasonal items. |
| Loot cases and card sets | Mixed, some harm signal | Limit. Earned only, capped per day, odds shown, and direct-purchase route for cards or duplicate exchange. |

Metrics to watch for a group this small: weekly active friends, median and top-holder balances, sessions after a loss, time played at night, limit use, and direct feedback at each season's end.

### Gaps
- I did not research the effect of paying currency for school work (motivation or cheating risk). Only Bahlou's own context raises it, so treat it separately.
- None of this evidence was collected from closed groups of 7-30 friends. All adaptation to that size is reasoning, so a season-by-season review with the group itself is the best available test.
